Definitions & Glossary

Simple Guide to How Ultrasonic Navigation Works

Wave Physics

4 Terms

Specular Loss (Deflection)

Pulse-Echo

When sound hits a very smooth, angled surface—like a glass door at a slant—it bounces away like a flashlight beam off a mirror instead of returning to the sensor, making the glass invisible to the ultrasonic beam.

Incidence Angle (θi)

Acoustics

The slant angle at which a sound wave hits a wall or object. Hitting straight-on sends a strong echo back; hitting at a steep angle causes the sound to bounce sideways.

Echo Intensity (Ir)

Acoustics

How loud and clear the returning echo is. Hard, flat walls send back a strong echo, while soft materials or angled surfaces send back a weak one.

Beam Cone Spread (Φ)

Pulse-Echo

How wide the sensor's invisible sound cone spreads out in front of the user (30° by default)—wider cones spot obstacles off to the sides, while narrower cones focus straight ahead.

Navigation & Cane

4 Terms

Tactile Hit

Hazard

Physically tapping an obstacle at ground level with a traditional white cane (within a ~1.1 m sweep) to feel what is directly in front of your feet.

Head / Chest Overhang

Hazard

Objects sticking out at upper-body height—like low tree branches, wall-mounted signs, or open truck tailgates—that a ground cane passes right underneath without touching, but an ultrasonic beam catches early.

Low Curb / Step

Hazard

Small height changes on the ground like sidewalk edges or stairs. Because they sit low to the floor, a white cane detects them reliably even when a chest-level ultrasonic sensor might miss them.

Blindfold Mode

Pulse-Echo

A training mode that hides the visual floorplan so you can test navigating purely by listening to the stereo proximity beeps and voice warnings.

Signals & Audio

3 Terms

Trigger vs. Echo Pulse

Audio

A live timing chart comparing two signals: the Trigger (when the sensor sends out a tiny 40 kHz ultrasonic chirp) and the Echo (when the bounce-back arrives). A wider gap between them means the obstacle is farther away.

Stereo Proximity Pings

Audio

Audio beeps that speed up and rise in pitch as you get closer to an object, shifting between your left and right ear to show which side the obstacle is on.

Material Acoustic Impedance

Audio

How much a material resists or absorbs sound waves—hard concrete bounces sound right back, while soft curtains soak up the sound like a sponge.

EchoSim — Ultrasonic Smart Navigation Emulator

The Real-World Problem

A traditional white cane is great for finding curbs and obstacles on the ground (within about 1.1 meters), but it cannot detect hazards at head or chest height—like low tree branches, wall-mounted signs, or open tailgates—before a person walks into them. EchoSim shows how a simple ultrasonic sensor (like the HC-SR04 used in student hardware builds) adds an early-warning zone up to 3.5 meters ahead.

How Pulse-Echo Sensing Works (Step by Step)

  • 1. Send a Sound Pulse: The sensor sends out a short, silent ultrasonic chirp (at 40 kHz, above human hearing) in a cone ahead of the user.
  • 2. Bounce Off Objects: When the sound wave hits a wall, person, or overhang, it bounces back as an echo.
  • 3. Measure the Gap: By timing how long the echo takes to return, the system calculates the exact distance to the obstacle.
  • 4. Alert the User: That distance is converted into faster audio beeps, left/right stereo cues, and clear voice warnings.

Why Combine a White Cane with Ultrasonic Sensing?

  • Better Coverage: The cane protects the ground path (curbs and steps), while the ultrasonic beam scans forward at chest and head level.
  • Spotting Sensor Limits: EchoSim also demonstrates real edge cases—such as smooth angled glass deflecting sound away or soft curtains absorbing echoes—proving why both tools work best together.

Gives the strongest, most reliable distance reading.
